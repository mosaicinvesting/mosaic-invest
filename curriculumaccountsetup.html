Course 3

Opening Your Account.

A real, screenshot-by-screenshot walkthrough of opening a custodial brokerage account. Pairs with Module 4 of Investing Foundations.

Before you start

A custodial account is opened and legally owned by a parent or guardian on a minor's behalf, and control transfers to the teen once they reach the age of majority in their state. A parent or guardian has to be the one to open it, so this isn't something you can set up entirely alone. We've linked two real starting points below; their screens won't look identical, so treat the steps that follow as a guide to the shape of the process, not an exact match for either one.

Charles Schwab custodial account ↗ Fidelity custodial account ↗
1

Start with the overview

Go to your brokerage's custodial account page (Schwab and Fidelity are linked above) and find the button to open an account. This is also where you can see what the account offers before committing to anything.

step-1-overview.png Screenshot the custodial account overview page, including the "Open an account" button.
2

The parent or guardian starts the account

A custodial account is opened by the parent or guardian, often with their existing brokerage login. Some brokerages check whether they already have an account and prompt them to open one first if not.

step-2-parent-account.png Screenshot the screen that checks for or asks about a parent/guardian account.
3

Enter the teen's information

This step asks for basic information: name, date of birth, and address.

Before you screenshot: blur or crop out the date of birth, Social Security number, and home address before this goes anywhere public.
step-3-teen-info.png Screenshot the teen info form, with date of birth and any ID numbers blurred.
4

Verify identity

Because of anti-money-laundering rules, the brokerage has to confirm who you are. This usually means uploading a photo ID, and sometimes an image of a Social Security card.

step-4-identity.png Screenshot the identity verification / document upload screen (blank form is fine, no need to include your actual ID).
5

Fund the account

Link a bank account or make a transfer to add money. Most custodial accounts have no minimum, so this step doesn't take much.

step-5-funding.png Screenshot the funding screen, showing the option to link a bank or transfer money.
6

Review and submit

Double check everything before submitting. Once it's in, the brokerage reviews the application.

Before you screenshot: crop out or blur the account number on the confirmation screen.
step-6-review.png Screenshot the review/confirmation screen, with the account number blurred.
7

Activate the account

Once approved, the account is finalized and ready to fund and use. Some brokerages also let the teen set up their own login to view the account, though the parent or guardian stays in legal control until the teen reaches the age of majority.

step-7-activate.png Screenshot the activation/confirmation screen once the account is approved.
8

Explore the dashboard and place your first trade

From here you can see the account, search for a stock or fund, and place an order. Good moment to go back to Module 4 of Investing Foundations and think about market vs. limit orders before you click buy.

Before you screenshot: blur or crop out the account balance and account number.
step-8-dashboard.png Screenshot the dashboard or trade ticket, with balance and account number blurred.

If you use a different brokerage

Every brokerage's flow looks a little different, but the shape is the same: prove who you are, add money, and you're set up to invest. Look for these same checkpoints, verifying identity, funding, and activation, wherever you open an account.
